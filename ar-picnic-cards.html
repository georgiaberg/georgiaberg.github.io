Games on a
blanket.

Twenty-eight authentic relating games, rewritten so a stranger can pick one up off a picnic blanket and run it without you standing over them.

Setting up

  • Print single-sided, cut along the dashed lines. You get 28 half-page cards.
  • Lay them out in four piles by tier, left to right, in the order they're printed. The order is the arc of the afternoon.
  • Put a hand-written sign on each pile with the group size range, so people can self-sort fast.
  • Cardstock survives grass and wind. Regular paper needs a rock on each pile.

The only three things you say out loud

  • One person reads the whole card aloud before anyone starts. Including the closing line at the bottom.
  • Passing is always fine — on a question, on a turn, on a whole game. No one explains why.
  • Someone runs the timer. Nobody's calling time for you. Nominate a person per group.

What the pips mean

Silly. No self-disclosure required.
You'll say true things about yourself.
You may get seen more than you planned.

A shape that works for a drop-in picnic

  • First hour: only Tier 1 and 2 on the blanket. People are arriving, eating, and won't commit to a pair exercise with food in their hands.
  • Once there's a crowd: call one whole-group game yourself — Rock Paper Scissors Tournament is the best cold-open ever invented. It gives everyone permission to be loud in public.
  • Mid-afternoon: put out Tier 3. Groups will have found each other by now. Don't announce it, just add the pile.
  • Before people drift off: Tier 4. If you only do one, do Appreciation Dyads.

Deliberately left out

The manual has excellent games I didn't include, because they need a trained facilitator watching, an established group, or both: Doom Circle, Fly on the Wall, Impression Spectrum, Kiss Kiss Diss, Anonymous Confessions, Withholds, Progressive Touch, Angel Walk, Try Me On, Shadow Operation. Handed to strangers with no one holding the room, these go wrong. Leave them out of the pile.

Touch

Five cards involve touch, and they're deliberately spread across the range. Race Cars and The Stick Game are physical but not intimate — hands on shoulders, a stick between two fingertips. Reach Out and Touch My Elbow are slower and need both people to actually want them. Unconditional Hugs is the warmest and has the clearest opt-out built into it. Every one of them can be declined mid-game by lowering your hands, and every card says so.

Rock Paper Scissors Tournament

People6–40
Time5–10 min
SpaceRoom to mill about
Someone read this out loud
  • Find anyone. Play one round of rock paper scissors.
  • The loser does not leave. They stand behind the winner and become their loudest, most undignified fan — chanting their name, cheering every round.
  • Winners go find other winners. Whoever loses, their whole entourage joins the other person's entourage.
  • Crowds merge. Eventually two enormous mobs face off for the final. Everyone screams.
End it hereChampion takes a victory lap. Everyone applauds. Play again or move on.

I'm a Tree!

People5–15
Time10–15 min
SpaceStanding circle
Someone read this out loud
  • One person steps into the middle: "I'm a tree," and makes a tree shape.
  • Two more people step in and add to the scene, saying what they are and holding the shape. "I'm an apple." "I'm a squirrel."
  • The tree picks one to stay: "The squirrel stays." The other two sit down.
  • The survivor restates what they are — "I'm a squirrel" — and two new people join. Repeat.
  • It really opens up when people go abstract: "I'm the squirrel's fear of commitment."
End it hereWhenever the laughing peaks. Don't wait for a natural stopping point; there isn't one.

Best Friends & Mortal Enemies

People8–30
Time5 min
SpaceOpen grass
Someone read this out loud
  • Everyone spread out and wander. Silently pick one person to be your best friend. Don't tell them, don't look at them too hard.
  • On the count of three — get as close to your best friend as you possibly can. Three, two, one, go.
  • Now silently pick a mortal enemy. On three, get as far away from them as you can.
  • Final round: get your best friend between you and your enemy. Go.
End it hereWhen everyone's out of breath. Reveal your picks if you want; it's funnier if you don't.

Whatcha Doin'?

People3–10
Time5–10 min
SpaceCircle, sitting or standing
Someone read this out loud
  • One person starts miming an activity — swinging a bat, folding laundry, whatever.
  • The person next to them asks: "Whatcha doin'?"
  • The mimer answers with something completely different from what they're actually doing. "I'm brushing my teeth."
  • Now the asker starts miming that — brushing teeth. The next person around asks "Whatcha doin'?" and gets another mismatched answer.
  • Answer fast. If you're thinking, you're too slow.
End it hereAfter a lap or two. Works in pairs too, back and forth.

Two Three-Minute Fillers

People3–12
Time3–5 min each
SpaceAny circle
Someone read this out loud
  • Fortune Cookie. Go around the circle. Each person says one word, building a single sentence together. When the sentence lands, that's the group's fortune. Read it back.
  • Word Association. One word each, as fast as you can, whatever the last person's word pulled up. No censoring, no explaining. If you hesitate more than two seconds, just say the boring word.
  • Harder version: each word has to be something you're noticing or feeling right now, and the next person associates off that.
End it hereTwo or three rounds. These are palate cleansers, not main courses.

How's Yours?

People5–15
Time10–20 min
SpaceCircle + somewhere to step away
Someone read this out loud
  • One person walks out of earshot.
  • Everyone else agrees on a thing they all have. Concrete: a toothbrush, a front door, a phone. Or abstract: childhood memories, a worst habit, a recurring worry. Abstract is more interesting.
  • They come back. They can ask only one question, to anyone, in any order: "How's yours?"
  • Answer honestly but vaguely — one or two words. "Chipped." "Loud." "Neglected."
  • They keep asking until they guess it. Then they pick who steps away next.
End it hereAfter three or four rounds, or when someone picks something impossible and the group revolts.

Race Cars

PeoplePairs
Time7–10 min
SpaceClear open grass
Someone read this out loud
  • A closes their eyes. B stands behind them and puts both hands on A's shoulders. A is the car, B is the driver.
  • A starts walking forward. B steers. B's entire job is not driving A into a tree.
  • Start in first gear. After a bit, shift into second. Then third. Then fourth, at which point everyone is more or less running with their eyes shut.
  • Slow down, come to a stop, swap roles.
  • Cruel and excellent variation: swap drivers mid-lap without telling the car.
  • Check the ground first — blankets, food, dogs, small children.
End it hereTrade partners and go again. Best played by whoever is currently the most stuck in their head.

The Stick Game

PeoplePairs
Time10 min
SpaceAnywhere · find a stick
Someone read this out loud
  • Each pair finds a stick about a hand's length. A pen works. Two index fingers pressed together works.
  • One fingertip each on either end. Now move around without dropping it. Walk, turn, crouch, go wherever.
  • After a few minutes, swap who's leading and who's following.
  • Then play with no assigned leader at all and see whether you can still tell who's deciding.
  • Harder: eyes closed. Or balance it between noses, elbows, shoulders.
  • Any pair that hasn't dropped it once should be given a second stick immediately.
End it hereSwap partners. It's a completely different game with someone else.

One Time…

People3–8 (4 is ideal)
Time10 min – an hour
SpaceSitting anywhere
Someone read this out loud
  • Take turns telling one-sentence true stories that start with "One time…"
  • A comma is allowed. A semicolon is pushing it. A paragraph is cheating. Keep them hot and compressed: "One time I got locked in a museum after closing."
  • Go around to the left, poker-style.
  • Don't prepare while someone else is talking. Listen to them, and then notice what story surfaces. That's the actual practice here.
  • Optional theme for a round: fear, desire, money, things I don't tell my family, the worst thing I've eaten.
End it hereLast lap, each person adds: "…and remembering that now, I feel ___."

The Google Game

People2–5
Time10–20 min
SpaceSitting anywhere
Someone read this out loud
  • Pick someone and search them: "Priya, if I Googled you and sunburn, what would I find?"
  • Priya answers with whatever actually comes up in her memory — a story, an image, a feeling about sunburns. Not a fact, not a definition. Whatever's on the first page.
  • Then someone else searches someone else. Any noun works. Weirder nouns work better.
  • Double-click: if something in their answer catches you, say "double-click 'my grandmother'" and they open that one up.
  • Optional: pick a theme and run all searches through it — shame, home, luck, money.
End it hereWhenever. This one also survives being played on a long drive.

Social Sentence Stems

People3–20
Time10–25 min
SpaceAny circle
Someone read this out loud
  • One person invents a sentence stem — an unfinished sentence they'd like to hear everyone answer.
  • Go around the circle. Everyone completes it, saying their name first if people don't all know each other. The person who chose the stem answers last.
  • Then the next person around invents a stem. Continue until everyone's had a turn choosing.
  • Stems to steal if you're stuck: Something I've changed my mind about is… / The last time I surprised myself was… / What I want more of this year is… / Something I'm bad at that I keep doing anyway is… / Right now I'm quietly excited about…
End it hereWhen the circle's gone round once. Or don't — this can run all afternoon.

Anybody Else?

People8–25
Time15–20 min
SpaceCircle on blankets
Someone read this out loud
  • One person stands and says something true about themselves, then asks: "Anybody else?"
  • Everyone it's also true for stands up, looks around at who else is standing, and sits back down. That look is the whole point.
  • Next person stands and shares. Keep going.
  • Start light and let it find its own level: "I'm the oldest sibling." "I've never actually read the book I say is my favourite." "I've cried this week." "I'm nervous right now."
  • No long stories — one sentence, something that could be true for someone else too.
End it hereWhen the energy flags, or after everyone's shared once. Standing version: swap in "Walk with me if…" and everyone it's true for walks a lap together.

Curiosity

PeoplePairs
Time20–25 min
SpaceSit facing each other
Someone read this out loud · Set a timer
  • Pick an A and a B. 5 min: A asks B anything at all. The only rule — A has to actually want to know the answer. No polite questions. B can decline any question without explaining.
  • 1 min: B gives feedback. What landed, what didn't, anything you wished had been asked.
  • 3 min: A asks again, with that information.
  • Then A gives three gifts, one sentence each: "My first impression of you was…" · "I felt you most when…" · "What I really get about you is…"
  • Swap roles and run it again.
End it hereOptional: B replies with "What I want you to get about me is…" before you swap.

Cartography

PeoplePairs
Time20 min
SpaceSit facing each other
Someone read this out loud · Set a timer
  • Pick an A and a B. 7 min: B asks A only hypothetical questions. "Would you ever…?" "Would you rather…?" "Would you like to…?"
  • A answers honestly, adding a condition where it's needed: "Yes, if I knew nobody would find out."
  • Go for breadth, not depth. Collect as many landmarks as possible. Don't get stuck exploring one.
  • 2 min: B says "What I think I get about you is…" — hand over the map you drew.
  • 2 min: A says "What I realize about myself is…" — fill it in.
  • Swap and repeat.
End it hereBoth of you will leave with a list of things you didn't know you had opinions about.

Noticing

PeoplePairs
Time5–15 min
SpaceSit facing each other
Someone read this out loud
  • A starts: "Sitting here with you, I notice…" and names one thing from their experience right now.
  • B answers: "Hearing that, I notice…"
  • Back and forth, same two phrases, for a few minutes. Keep eye contact if you can.
  • B's noticing doesn't have to relate to A's. The phrase just keeps you in relationship. What matters is that it's happening right now, not five minutes ago.
  • Try a round of each: a physical sensation · an emotion · a thought · something directly observable about the other person.
End it hereHarder and better version: "I notice [something observable about you]… and I imagine…" Then ask them: "Is that true?"

What's That Like?

PeoplePairs
Time10 min
SpaceSit facing each other
Someone read this out loud · Set a timer
  • Pick an A and a B. A says what they're feeling right now, or whatever's on top for them.
  • B's only move is to ask a version of "What's that like?" That's it. "What's it like to feel that?" "Where is that in your body?"
  • No advice. No stories of your own. No fixing. No changing the subject. B is a mirror, not a participant.
  • B can point at contradictions: "What's it like to say that and be smiling?"
  • 3–5 minutes, then swap.
  • It will feel weirdly restrictive around minute two. Stay in the format anyway — that's the whole exercise.
End it hereSit quietly for thirty seconds before you start talking normally again.

Empathy

PeoplePairs (or 3)
Time15–20 min
SpaceSit facing each other
Someone read this out loud · Set a timer
  • Pick an A and a B. Start with a few seconds of silent eye contact.
  • 2 min: A shares something that's on their heart.
  • 1 min: B reflects back exactly what they heard, with as little interpretation as they can manage. Not a summary — a mirror.
  • 2 min: A corrects, adds, or goes further.
  • 2 min: B shares what they felt listening, and what they get about A.
  • Swap. Don't compose your reflection while they're talking — you'll miss them, and they'll know.
End it hereWith three: C reflects second, after B. Trust the format; the silence between steps is doing work.

I've Felt That

PeoplePairs
Time10–15 min
SpaceSit facing each other
Someone read this out loud
  • A names three emotions they felt at one single moment in their life. "Relieved, ashamed, and very awake."
  • No story. No context. No explaining. Just the three words.
  • B sits with them for a moment, then tells a story of a time they felt those same three.
  • A says what felt similar, what felt different, what surprised them.
  • Swap.
End it hereOptional: A can then tell their own story, now that B has told theirs. Often it's completely different and lands anyway.

Hot Seat

People4–8
Time15–40 min
SpaceCircle with one seat in front
Someone read this out loud
  • Someone volunteers for the seat and announces their heat: mild, medium, or spicy. The group calibrates its questions to that. Believe them when they say mild.
  • The group asks questions for 4–10 minutes.
  • Ask interested questions, not interesting ones. An interested question keeps the light on them. An interesting question is secretly about you.
  • They can decline anything, no reason given.
  • Anyone can say "thank you" mid-answer, which means stop talking now — even mid-sentence. It keeps answers hot instead of rambling.
  • Whoever's holding the card ends it: "…and we'll take you off the hot seat there." End on a high note, not a low one.
End it hereGo round the group with "What I get about you is…" before the next person sits down. Don't skip this.

Rooms of the Heart

People3–4
Time20–30 min
SpaceSmall circle
Someone read this out loud
  • Pick who goes first. They finish this sentence: "If my heart had different rooms, the one I spend most time in is…" — a playful room, a working room, a waiting room, whatever they've got.
  • 4 min: the group gets curious about that room. Ask questions. What's in it? Who else is allowed in? When were they last there?
  • They also say what it's like having these particular people in the room with them right now.
  • 4 min: everyone else answers — "I have a room like that too, and mine is…"
  • Move to the next person.
End it hereOnce everyone's shown a room. Nobody has to pick the deepest one they've got.

Who Are You?

PeoplePairs
Time15 min
SpaceSit facing each other
Someone read this out loud · Set a timer
  • Pick an A and a B.
  • A asks: "Who are you?" — B answers for 2 minutes. Keep going past the obvious answers; that's where it starts.
  • A asks: "Who do you pretend to be?" — B answers for 2 minutes.
  • Now B asks A: "Who do you think I am?" — A answers for 2 minutes, honestly, from the last few minutes of evidence.
  • A minute or two to talk about what that was like. Then swap roles and run the whole thing again.
End it hereThe third question is the one people remember. Don't rush it, and don't be polite in it.

Reach Out

PeoplePairs
Time5–10 min
SpaceSit facing each other
Someone read this out loud · Both of you have to want this
  • Sit facing each other. This whole game is nonverbal.
  • A minute or more: connect using fingertips only. Play, follow, lead, rest. See what a very small amount of contact actually feels like.
  • Then: whole hands.
  • Then: hands and forearms.
  • Try a round with eyes closed and a round with eyes open. Notice which one you avoided.
  • Either person can stop at any point by lowering their hands. No explanation needed and none asked for.
End it hereTalk about it afterwards. Two minutes each on what you noticed in your own body.

Touch My Elbow

PeoplePairs
Time10–15 min
SpaceSitting anywhere
Someone read this out loud · Both of you have to want this
  • Whoever has the longest sleeves goes first.
  • Take turns making a request in this exact form: "I'd like my ___ to be touched." Be specific. Elbow, shoulder blade, left ear, back of the hand.
  • Your partner either lightly touches that spot, or declines. Both are completely ordinary answers and neither one needs a reason.
  • Twister rule: once you're touching a spot, keep touching it until you need that hand somewhere else.
  • If they decline, you touch that spot yourself.
  • If you'd rather not be touched on skin, name a piece of clothing — a sleeve, a collar, a shoelace. That's a full answer, not a lesser one.
End it hereThe game is really about asking for something precisely, and finding out that a no costs nothing.

Unconditional Hugs

People10–24
Time10–15 min
SpaceTwo circles
Someone read this out loud · All of it, before anyone moves
  • Half the group stands in an outer circle facing in. The other half forms an inner circle facing out.
  • The outer circle closes their eyes.
  • The consent signal: if you don't want to be hugged, stay in the circle with your eyes closed and wrap your own arms around yourself. That's it. Nobody asks about it, nobody comments on it, and you can put your arms up or down at any point.
  • The inner circle wanders and hugs whoever is open. Tap them on the arm or shoulder first so nobody gets startled.
  • Play it silently — that's what keeps the hugger anonymous, and it's why the game works.
  • After a few minutes, swap which circle has their eyes closed.
End it hereTalk in pairs about what it was like to receive without knowing who from.

Appreciation Dyads

PeoplePairs
Time10–12 min
SpaceSit facing each other
Someone read this out loud · Set a timer
  • Pick an A and a B. 5 minutes each on one sentence stem, over and over:
  • "Being with you, I appreciate…"
  • It can be about them, about yourself, or about what's happening between you. It has to be about right now, not about the general idea of them.
  • Five minutes is longer than it sounds. The first two run dry, and then it gets good — the digging for something new is where the connection actually happens. Don't stop early.
  • Swap. If there's time, swap partners entirely and go again.
End it hereSay thank you and mean it. This is a good last thing to do before people start packing up.

The Love Blaster

People4+ (builds 1 → 2 → 4)
Time15 min
SpaceAnywhere with objects
Someone read this out loud
  • Alone, 2 min: pick any object near you — a leaf, a shoe, a thermos. Appreciate everything about it. Then close your eyes and notice what appreciating feels like from the inside.
  • In pairs, 3 min: pick a new object together. Appreciate it out loud to each other, like you're two insufferable people at an art gallery.
  • Find another pair. 2 min: you two whisper to each other about them — everything you notice and appreciate — while they do the same about you. Gossip, but nice.
  • 3 min: now say all of it out loud, to their faces.
End it hereClose your eyes for thirty seconds and notice the feeling of appreciation still sitting in you.

Shower of Love

People5–20
Time1–2 min per person
SpaceAny circle
Someone read this out loud · Set a timer
  • One person at a time is the focus. They just sit there and take it.
  • Everyone else calls out single words or short phrases — a quality they see, an impact this person has, something they've noticed today.
  • Anyone can speak, in any order, over each other. You can repeat a word someone else said if you agree with it. Let it overlap into a collage.
  • Set a timer for 90 seconds. When it goes, whoever's holding the card says "thank you" and you move to the next person.
  • The timer matters. Without it this either drifts or gets uneven, and uneven is worse.
End it hereEveryone gets a turn or nobody does. Don't start unless there's time for the whole circle.

Like, Like, GOOSE!

People8–20
Time15–20 min
SpaceCircle on open grass
Someone read this out loud
  • Everyone sits in a circle. One person walks around the outside.
  • They lightly touch each person's head or shoulder and say one word they like about that person. "Curious." "Warm." "Loud."
  • When they're ready, instead of a compliment they shout "GOOSE!" — and run.
  • The goose chases them around the circle. If the goose tags them before they reach the empty spot, the walker goes again. If not, the goose becomes the new appreciator.
  • If you don't know someone well, say what you've noticed in the last hour. It works fine.
End it hereWhen people are lying on the grass laughing and nobody wants to run any more. That's the picnic over.
